Lean Systems · Value Stream Mapping

How do you find waste in a factory the size of three football fields?

You can't see it by walking around. Everyone looks busy.

Lean companies use an X-ray of the whole process: a Value Stream Map (VSM). It follows one product from the customer's order all the way to delivery.

Before we can read one, we need three words. Let's start with a burger.

Word 1 of 3

Cycle time: the actual work

You're standing in the In-N-Out kitchen with a stopwatch. The cook puts a patty on the grill: start. Cooked, on the bun, wrapped, handed to the window: stop.

Raw patty→Grill→Assemble→Wrap=2 minutes
Cycle time (C/T): the time it takes to do the value-adding work on one unit. Hands on the product.
Word 2 of 3

Lead time: the customer's reality

Now step outside. You pull into the back of the drive-thru line. You order. You wait. You inch forward. You wait some more. Finally: food in hand.

🚗 Join the line→Order→Wait…→Window=15 minutes
Lead time: the total time the customer experiences, from order to delivery. It includes all the waiting, not just the work.
Word 3 of 3

Work-in-process: the traffic jam

The burger takes 2 minutes to make. Why did you wait 15? Where did the other 13 go?

You were waiting behind every other half-finished order already in the system.

Work-in-process (WIP): partly finished units sitting between steps. WIP is the traffic jam that stretches lead time.
2 min
13 min waiting

Work = 2 ÷ 15 = 13.3% of your time. Waiting = 13 ÷ 15 = 86.7%.

The core lesson

Which number would you attack?

Traditional manager

"Flip the burgers faster! Buy a faster grill! Shave 10 seconds off cook time!"

Attacks the 2 minutes.

Lean manager

"Why spend money to save 10 seconds of work when the customer waits 13 minutes?"

Attacks the 13 minutes.

A VSM makes the traffic jam visible, so you stop optimizing the wrong thing.

Anatomy of a VSM

Every value stream map has three layers

1 · The Brain
top of the page		Information flow: orders, forecasts, schedules. How the company thinks.
2 · The Body
middle		Material flow: process boxes, data boxes, inventory piles. The physical floor.
3 · The Truth
bottom		The timeline: work time vs. waiting time. The reason the map exists.

A flowchart only shows "what happens next." A VSM tracks information moving backward and material moving forward, at the same time.

Layer 1 · The Brain

Information flow

🌾 Supplier⇠Production Control⇠🏪 Customer

The customer sits on the right, the supplier on the left, and the planning office in the middle. Arrows show orders, forecasts, and schedules: straight lines for manual, zig-zags for electronic.

The customer changes an order Tuesday, but the floor doesn't hear until Thursday. What happens on Wednesday?

Bad information creates physical waste. The floor spends Wednesday making the wrong product: overproduction.

Layer 3 · The Truth

The staircase timeline

High steps = waiting, in days. Low steps = work, in seconds.

The work happens in seconds. The waiting happens in days.
